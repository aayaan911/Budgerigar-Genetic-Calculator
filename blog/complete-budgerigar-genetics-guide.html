What budgerigar genetics is and why it matters

Budgerigar genetics is the systematic study of inheritance patterns for the colour and pattern mutations that produce the rainbow of variants seen in modern budgerigar (budgie) breeding. A wild budgerigar is Light Green, the ancestral phenotype found in Australian outback flocks. Every other colour and pattern combination found in captive birds today arose from heritable mutations to specific genes that breeders selected and stabilised over the last 175 years.

Understanding budgerigar genetics matters for three reasons. First, breeders who pair birds without knowing what each parent carries get unpredictable offspring and frequently destroy expensive bloodlines through unintended outcrosses. Second, exhibition breeders need to know exactly what genes their birds carry to plan the multi-generation projects required for serious show competition. Third, pet keepers who want a specific colour or pattern need to know what to ask for when buying or breeding.

The three inheritance types in budgerigar genetics

Every budgerigar mutation falls into one of three inheritance types. The type determines exactly how the mutation is passed from parents to chicks, which is the foundation of all genetic prediction.

Sex-linked recessive mutations sit on the Z chromosome. Budgerigars use a ZW sex determination system where cocks are ZZ (two Z chromosomes) and hens are ZW (one Z and one W). A sex-linked recessive gene is hidden on the Z chromosome. Cocks need two copies (one on each Z) to express the mutation visually. Hens only have one Z chromosome so they either show the mutation visually or do not have it at all. Hens cannot be split for sex-linked recessive mutations. The six sex-linked recessive budgerigar mutations are Opaline, Cinnamon, Ino (Lutino on green, Albino on blue), Lacewing, Slate, and Texas Clearbody.

Autosomal recessive mutations sit on autosomal chromosomes, not the sex chromosomes. Cocks and hens inherit them identically. A bird needs two copies of the gene (one from each parent) to express the mutation visually. A bird with one copy is split and looks like a Normal bird but passes the gene to half its offspring. The ten autosomal recessive budgerigar mutations are Recessive Pied, German Fallow, English Fallow, Scottish Fallow, Clearwing, Greywing, Dilute, Black Face, Blackwing, and Saddleback.

Autosomal incompletely dominant mutations sit on autosomal chromosomes and produce a partial phenotype with one copy (Single Factor or SF) and a stronger phenotype with two copies (Double Factor or DF). Both sexes inherit identically and SF birds are visually distinguishable from DF birds because the dominance is incomplete. The eight autosomal incompletely dominant budgerigar mutations are Grey Factor, Violet, Yellow Face, Goldenface (covered in the Yellow Face article), Spangle, Dominant Pied, Clearflight Pied, Anthracite, and Easley Clearbody. Dark Factor is also autosomal incompletely dominant but is built into the base colour name rather than treated as a separate mutation.

Sex-linked recessive mutations on the Z chromosome

All six sex-linked recessive mutations share the same fundamental inheritance pattern. Cocks have two Z chromosomes so they can be visual, split, or completely free of any sex-linked gene. Hens have one Z chromosome so they are either visual or completely free of the gene. Hens cannot be split for any sex-linked mutation.

This sex-linked inheritance supports the famous auto-sex pairing trick. A visible sex-linked recessive cock paired with a Normal hen produces 100 percent visible daughters and 100 percent split sons. The daughters can be sexed at hatch by colour because the sex-linked recessive gene that the cock passes to his daughters expresses visually on their single Z chromosome.

Opaline originated in Australia 1933 and produces the wing-reversal pattern with colour distributed across the body in a distinctive way. The most-bred budgerigar mutation worldwide.

Cinnamon reduces black wing markings to soft cinnamon brown. The TRP1 gene controls this melanin alternative pathway.

Ino removes all melanin pigmentation, producing all-yellow Lutino on green-series birds, all-white Albino on blue-series birds, and red eyes in both cases.

Lacewing is not a separate gene but the Z-chromosome crossover product of Cinnamon and Ino, per Inte Onsman MUTAVI Research 2007. A Lacewing bird carries both genes on the same Z chromosome due to chromosomal recombination.

Slate adds a slate-grey wash on blue-series birds. UK 1950s origin. Rare worldwide.

Texas Clearbody lightens the body while keeping head and wing markings dark. Allelic with Ino at the ino-locus (TCB dominant over Ino on the Z chromosome). Texas USA 1950s origin.

Autosomal recessive mutations

Autosomal recessive mutations require two copies (one from each parent) to express visually. A bird with only one copy is split and looks Normal but passes the gene to half its offspring. Cocks and hens inherit identically.

Recessive Pied (also called Danish Recessive Pied) produces irregular yellow or white patches on the body. Denmark 1932 origin. Combines with Clearflight Pied to produce the emergent Dark-Eyed Clear (DEC) phenotype.

German Fallow originated in Germany 1929 (Schrapel) and is the oldest and most common of three canonical Fallow mutations. Visual birds show bronze body suffusion combined with red eyes that have a clearly visible white iris ring.

English Fallow originated in England 1937 and is the second canonical Fallow. Solid red eyes with no visible iris ring distinguish it from German Fallow. Also called Pale Fallow (per Onsman MUTAVI) or Dun Fallow.

Scottish Fallow originated in Scotland 1947 (W. Bryce) and is the rarest canonical Fallow. Pink iris colour distinguishes it from the red-eyed German and English variants. All three Fallow types are non-allelic with each other, so cross-Fallow pairings produce Normal-looking split-both chicks, not visible Fallow chicks.

The dil-locus allelic series consists of three mutations sharing the same locus. Clearwing (Yellow-Wing on green, White-Wing on blue) is dominant over Greywing and Dilute. Greywing produces 50 percent diluted wing markings and is dominant over Dilute. Dilute (Suffused Yellow/White) is recessive to both Clearwing and Greywing. Clearwing combined with Greywing produces the unique hetero-allelic Fullbody Greywing (FBG) phenotype, the most prized dil-locus combination in exhibition stock.

Black Face produces near-black face and throat from excess melanin. Netherlands 1992 (Van Dijk) origin. Blackwing produces thick black wing markings. Venezuela 2002 (Edixon Laya) origin. Combined together, Black Face and Blackwing produce the prestigious Double Black exhibition phenotype.

Saddleback produces a distinctive saddleback marking pattern. Australian origin.

Autosomal incompletely dominant mutations and Dark Factor

Autosomal incompletely dominant mutations produce a partial phenotype with one copy (Single Factor, SF) and a stronger phenotype with two copies (Double Factor, DF). Both cocks and hens express identically. SF and DF birds are visually distinguishable because the dominance is incomplete.

The Dark Factor is built into the base colour name rather than treated as a separate mutation. Light Green is the wild-type green base. Dark Green is Light Green plus one dark factor. Olive Green is Light Green plus two dark factors. Sky Blue is the wild-type blue base. Cobalt is Sky Blue plus one dark factor. Mauve is Sky Blue plus two dark factors. Crossing Cobalt with Cobalt produces the classic 1:2:1 Mendelian ratio of 25 percent Sky Blue, 50 percent Cobalt, 25 percent Mauve. The Dark Factor article covers the full mechanics.

Grey Factor produces a grey body wash. SF Grey on Sky Blue is the Grey budgerigar, SF Grey on Cobalt is Dark Grey. Different from sex-linked Slate.

Violet adds violet sheen, most prized on Cobalt as Violet Cobalt. SF and DF expressions differ markedly. Most striking variant in exhibition blue series.

Yellow Face and Goldenface add yellow on the face for blue-series birds. Goldenface is a deeper golden-yellow than Yellow Face. Both are masked on green-series birds because the existing green pigment overwhelms the yellow expression. This is a judge-validated rule implemented in the calculator.

Spangle produces reverse wing markings (SF) or near-clear body (DF). Australia 1978 (Melville) origin. The DF Spangle phenotype is sometimes confused with Ino but DF Spangle has normal dark eyes while Ino has red eyes.

Dominant Pied (Banded Pied) produces an irregular pied band across the body. Australia 1935 origin.

Clearflight Pied (Continental Clearflight Pied) produces clear flight feathers and tail. Belgium/Netherlands 1948 origin. Combined with visible Recessive Pied produces the emergent Dark-Eyed Clear (DEC) phenotype.

Anthracite darkens the body uniformly. Germany 1998 origin. SF is medium grey, DF is near black.

Easley Clearbody (Dominant Clearbody) lightens the body while keeping head and wings dark. California 1992 (Steve Easley) origin. Different from sex-linked Texas Clearbody despite the similar name. The two clearbody mutations are non-allelic.

The Manto Negro mutation (Brazilian Black Mantle) is autosomal dominant. Brazil 2021 (Ley H. Silva Filho) origin. Cannot hide as a split, every carrier visibly shows the trait. Won Best in Show at the 2025 SOCO Cup.

Base colours and the budgerigar colour wheel

Wild-type budgerigars are Light Green, a yellow body with green sheen and black wing markings. Every other budgerigar phenotype derives from mutations to this baseline.

The blue series originated when the Blue mutation appeared in 1878 in Belgium. Blue is autosomal recessive: a bird carrying two copies of the blue gene shows the Sky Blue phenotype with a white body and blue sheen (no yellow pigment). One copy of Blue paired with one wild-type allele produces a Normal-looking Light Green bird that is split for Blue. Green-series birds split for Blue are commonly bred for show.

All budgerigars are either green-series (wild-type or split for Blue) or blue-series (homozygous Blue, no yellow pigment). The base colour interacts with most other mutations to produce the final phenotype. Yellow Face is masked on green series. Most mutations show subtly different colour expression on green vs blue series.

The Dark Factor stacks with both green and blue series. Light Green plus one dark factor is Dark Green. Plus two dark factors is Olive Green. Sky Blue plus one dark factor is Cobalt. Plus two dark factors is Mauve. Cross-series birds split for Blue can show Light Green / Blue, Dark Green / Blue, or Olive Green / Blue genotypes. The calculator at budgerigargenetics.com handles all base colour and dark factor combinations.

Grey Factor produces a grey body wash on top of the blue series. Grey on Sky Blue is Grey, Grey on Cobalt is Dark Grey, Grey on Mauve is the deepest grey variant. Grey Factor combined with green series produces Grey Green, Dark Grey Green, and Olive Grey Green. The Grey Factor article covers details.

The dil-locus allelic series, Clearwing Greywing Dilute and FBG

Three of the autosomal recessive mutations form a single allelic series at the dil-locus. This is one of the two key allelic series in budgerigar genetics and the most important interaction set for serious dil-locus breeders.

Allele symbols: dil^cw for Clearwing, dil^gw for Greywing, and dil (lowercase, no superscript) for Dilute. Dominance order: Clearwing dominant over Greywing, Greywing dominant over Dilute, all three recessive to wild-type.

The critical interaction is Clearwing with Greywing. A bird with one Clearwing allele and one Greywing allele (dil^cw/dil^gw) does not show the dominant Clearwing phenotype, nor the recessive Greywing phenotype. Instead it shows the hetero-allelic Fullbody Greywing (FBG), a phenotype with the bright body of Clearwing combined with the faded wing markings of Greywing. This is the only co-dominant interaction at the dil-locus and one of the most prized phenotypes in exhibition stock.

FBG cannot breed true. FBG paired with FBG produces 25 percent Clearwing, 50 percent FBG, 25 percent Greywing (textbook 1:2:1 for a co-dominant cross). Breeders who want consistent FBG must accept this segregation in every clutch.

The full dil-locus pairing predictions form a 58-pairing reference covering every Clearwing, Greywing, Dilute, FBG, and Normal combination. The ino-locus allelic series, Texas Clearbody dominant over Ino

The second key allelic series in budgerigar genetics is the ino-locus on the Z chromosome. Two sex-linked recessive mutations share this locus: Texas Clearbody (TCB) dominant over Ino.

A cock with one TCB allele and one Ino allele on his two Z chromosomes shows visual Texas Clearbody (body lightens, head and wings stay dark) because TCB dominates over Ino. The bird is hetero-allelic at the ino-locus, ZZ with TCB^z/Ino^z genotype, and the visual phenotype is TCB split for Ino.

A hen carries only one Z chromosome so she can be either visual TCB or visual Ino but never both simultaneously. The ino-locus allelic series only matters for cocks who can carry both alleles on their two Z chromosomes.

The practical implication: a cock split TCB paired with a visible Ino hen can produce visual TCB sons (TCB allele on Z from dad plus TCB allele on Z from mum is impossible because mum is visual Ino) , actually the more careful statement is that a cock split TCB paired with visible Ino hen produces 50 percent split TCB sons and 50 percent visible Ino sons. Always test-cross to confirm the cock's genotype.

The Texas Clearbody article covers all ino-locus interactions including the distinguishing tests against the autosomal Easley Clearbody (which is at a completely different locus, non-allelic with the ino-locus).

Six judge-validated engine rules unique to this calculator

The Budgerigar Genetics Calculator at budgerigargenetics.com implements six engine rules validated by WBO Certified Judges (Abdelrahman Khedr Egypt, Md Mojammel Hossain Bangladesh, Dewan MD Salim Bangladesh). These rules cover edge cases that mathematically correct Mendelian genetics alone cannot predict.

Rule 1, Yellow Face and Goldenface masked on green series. Both Yellow Face and Goldenface are autosomal incompletely dominant genes that only express visually on blue-series birds. On green-series birds the gene is carried but the yellow expression is masked by the existing green pigment. The calculator labels these as carrying SF Yellow Face or carrying DF Goldenface parenthetically rather than prepending the mutation name to the green phenotype.

Rule 2, Opaline visually masks Black Face when both visible. When a chick has both visual Opaline and visual Black Face, the Opaline pattern dominates the visible phenotype. Output is labelled Opaline masking Black Face.

Rule 3, Lacewing equals Cinnamon plus Ino Z-chromosome crossover. Per Inte Onsman MUTAVI Research 2007 paper, Lacewing is not a separate gene but the recombination product of Cinnamon plus Ino on the same Z chromosome. A Lacewing hen passes a single Z chromosome carrying both Cinnamon and Ino to her sons. Sons inheriting this Z are split for both Cinnamon and Ino, not split for Lacewing.

Rule 4, Dil-locus co-dominance. Clearwing, Greywing, and Dilute are alleles at the same locus, not independent genes. The Clearwing plus Greywing combination produces the hetero-allelic Fullbody Greywing phenotype.

Rule 5, Ino-locus allelic series. Texas Clearbody and Ino are alleles at the same Z-chromosome locus. TCB is dominant over Ino. A bird heterozygous TCB/Ino is visually TCB and split for Ino.

Rule 6, Dark-Eyed Clear auto-detection. When Clearflight Pied (SF or DF) combines with visible Recessive Pied in the same bird, the result is automatically labelled Dark-Eyed Clear (SF) or Dark-Eyed Clear (DF). DEC is an emergent phenotype, not a separate mutation.

Emergent phenotypes, DEC, FBG, and Double Black

Three emergent phenotypes appear in budgerigar breeding when specific mutation combinations interact. These phenotypes are not separate mutations but predictable products of combining two or more existing mutations.

Dark-Eyed Clear (DEC) emerges when a bird carries both Clearflight Pied (SF or DF) and visible Recessive Pied at the same time. The combined effect produces an almost-entirely yellow or white bird with dark plum-coloured eyes. The dark eye colour distinguishes DEC from Lutino and Albino which have red eyes. DF Clearflight paired with visible Recessive Pied produces 100 percent DEC chicks, the cleanest production pairing.

Fullbody Greywing (FBG) emerges from the hetero-allelic combination of Clearwing and Greywing at the dil-locus. The bright body of Clearwing combined with the faded wing markings of Greywing produces a uniquely valued exhibition phenotype. Clearwing paired with Greywing produces 100 percent FBG chicks. FBG paired with FBG produces 25 percent Clearwing, 50 percent FBG, 25 percent Greywing.

Double Black emerges when a bird carries both visible Black Face and visible Blackwing. The combined dark phenotypes produce an extraordinarily melanic exhibition bird prized in modern shows. Double Black is the result of pairing visual Black Face Blackwing birds with each other across multiple generations.

The Manto Negro (Brazilian Black Mantle) phenotype is sometimes confused with Double Black but is a different mutation entirely. Manto Negro is autosomal dominant while Double Black is the combination of two autosomal recessive mutations.

The auto-sex pairing trick that lets you sex chicks at hatch

One of the most useful breeder techniques in budgerigar genetics is the auto-sex pairing trick that lets a breeder identify chick sex at hatch by colour alone, without DNA testing.

The rule: a visible sex-linked recessive cock paired with a Normal hen produces 100 percent visible daughters (showing the cock's sex-linked recessive mutation) and 100 percent split sons (visually normal cocks carrying the gene invisibly).

Example: Opaline cock paired with Normal hen produces all Opaline daughters (visibly showing the wing-reversal pattern) and all split Opaline sons (visually normal). At hatch, breeders can identify Opaline-patterned chicks as female and normal-patterned chicks as male.

This trick works for all six sex-linked recessive mutations: Opaline, Cinnamon, Ino, Lacewing, Slate, and Texas Clearbody. Each one supports the same pattern when the cock is visual and the hen is Normal.

The trick is particularly valuable for commercial breeders who need to sex chicks early without expensive DNA tests. Combined with sex-linked Opaline, the auto-sex pairing trick is the foundation of many modern budgerigar breeding programmes.

Three canonical Fallow mutations distinguished by iris colour

Many older budgerigar references treat Fallow as a single mutation. Modern genetics recognises three distinct Fallow mutations distinguished by iris colour and country of origin.

German Fallow originated in Germany 1929 (Schrapel) and shows red eyes with a clearly visible white iris ring. Most common Fallow worldwide. Body suffusion is the deepest bronze of the three variants.

English Fallow originated in England 1937 and shows solid red eyes with no visible iris ring or barely discernible iris. Body is paler than German Fallow. Also called Pale Fallow (per Onsman MUTAVI) or Dun Fallow.

Scottish Fallow originated in Scotland 1947 (W. Bryce) and shows a pink iris distinguishing it from the red-eyed German and English variants. Body is the lightest of the three. Rarest worldwide.

All three Fallow mutations are autosomal recessive but at different non-allelic loci. This is the most common breeder misconception about Fallows. Crossing a visible German Fallow with a visible English Fallow does NOT produce Fallow chicks. The cross produces 100 percent Normal-looking chicks split for both genes. Each Fallow gene needs its own homozygous pair to express visually.

This non-allelic relationship is critical for serious Fallow breeders. Maintaining separate German, English, and Scottish lines requires never crossing between the variants unless the breeder specifically wants split-both stock for a future Double Fallow project. Combinations, the heart of advanced budgerigar breeding

Most modern exhibition budgerigars carry multiple mutations stacked into a single bird. Understanding combination mechanics is the difference between casual and competitive breeding.

Sex-linked plus autosomal combinations are the simplest because the two inheritance types operate independently. An Opaline cock split Cinnamon paired with a Normal hen produces 50 percent Opaline daughters split Cinnamon and 50 percent Cinnamon daughters split Opaline (assuming the Opaline cock is split Cinnamon). The sex-linked and autosomal genes segregate independently.

Multi-mutation combinations are managed by tracking each mutation independently and combining their probabilities. Cobalt Spangle SF Opaline cock paired with a Cinnamon Light Green / Blue hen produces a complex mix of base colour outcomes and mutation outcomes that the calculator models exactly. Manual prediction without a calculator becomes impractical past three mutations.

Dark factor stacking is a separate combination layer. Any mutation can combine with one or two dark factors. The dark factor changes the base colour name (Light Green to Dark Green to Olive Green, or Sky Blue to Cobalt to Mauve) and the underlying mutation continues to express on the new dark-shifted base.

Pied combinations are particularly powerful because the three pied mutations (Recessive Pied, Dominant Pied, Clearflight Pied) are non-allelic at different loci. A bird can carry any combination of the three pied mutations simultaneously. The DEC emergent phenotype occurs when Clearflight Pied and Recessive Pied combine in the same bird.

Frequently asked questions about complete genetics guide

How many documented budgerigar mutations exist in 2026?

Twenty-four documented mutations are modelled by the Budgerigar Genetics Calculator at budgerigargenetics.com. Six are sex-linked recessive (Opaline, Cinnamon, Ino, Lacewing, Slate, Texas Clearbody), ten are autosomal recessive (Recessive Pied, German Fallow, English Fallow, Scottish Fallow, Clearwing, Greywing, Dilute, Black Face, Blackwing, Saddleback), and eight are autosomal incompletely dominant (Grey, Violet, Yellow Face, Goldenface, Spangle, Dominant Pied, Clearflight Pied, Anthracite, Easley Clearbody). Dark Factor is built into base colours and Manto Negro is autosomal dominant covered separately.

What are the three inheritance types in budgerigar genetics?

Sex-linked recessive mutations sit on the Z chromosome. Cocks can be visual, split, or free of the gene. Hens are either visual or free of the gene, never split. Autosomal recessive mutations sit on autosomal chromosomes. Cocks and hens inherit identically. A bird needs two copies to express visually, one copy makes it split. Autosomal incompletely dominant mutations also sit on autosomal chromosomes but produce a partial phenotype with one copy (SF) and stronger phenotype with two copies (DF). SF and DF are visually distinguishable.

What is the dil-locus allelic series?

Three autosomal recessive mutations share the dil-locus: Clearwing (dominant, allele dil^cw), Greywing (middle, allele dil^gw), Dilute (recessive, allele dil). Clearwing dominates over Greywing, Greywing dominates over Dilute. The critical interaction is Clearwing with Greywing, which produces the unique hetero-allelic Fullbody Greywing (FBG) phenotype prized in exhibition stock. FBG cannot breed true.

How do the three canonical Fallow mutations differ?

German Fallow (1929, allele pf-d): red eyes WITH visible white iris ring, deepest bronze body. English Fallow (1937, allele pf-e): solid red eyes with no visible iris ring, paler body, also called Pale Fallow. Scottish Fallow (1947, allele pf-s): pink iris (not red), lightest body, rarest worldwide. All three are autosomal recessive but at different non-allelic loci. Cross-Fallow pairings produce Normal-looking split-both chicks, NOT visible Fallow chicks. The iris colour is the reliable diagnostic feature.

What is the auto-sex pairing trick?

A visible sex-linked recessive cock paired with a Normal hen produces 100 percent visible daughters (showing the cock's sex-linked recessive mutation) and 100 percent split sons (visually normal cocks carrying the gene invisibly). At hatch, breeders can sex chicks by colour because the sex-linked recessive gene that the cock passes to his daughters expresses visually on their single Z chromosome. Works for all six sex-linked recessive mutations: Opaline, Cinnamon, Ino, Lacewing, Slate, Texas Clearbody.
